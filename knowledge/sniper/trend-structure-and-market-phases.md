# Trend structure and market phases — trade with the higher timeframe

*Studied by: sniper (SNIPER). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent sniper`.*

## Key principle

The single biggest, most durable edge in timing is aligning with the
higher-timeframe trend. Markets move in recognizable structure — a series of
higher highs and higher lows in an uptrend, lower highs and lower lows in a
downtrend, and directionless overlap in a range. Fighting that structure
("picking tops and bottoms") is how market timers bleed out. The tape tells you
which phase you are in; discipline is trading with it, not against it.

## Concrete anchors

- **Dow theory / structure.** A trend is intact while it keeps making higher
  highs and higher lows (or the inverse); the trend is questioned only when
  that sequence breaks. Structure change — not a single candle — is the signal.
- **Wyckoff phases.** Price cycles through accumulation (smart money building),
  markup, distribution (smart money exiting), and markdown. Recognizing the
  phase tells you whether breakouts should be trusted or faded.
- **Timeframe alignment.** An entry is highest-probability when the lower
  timeframe setup points the same direction as the higher timeframe trend;
  counter-trend entries carry structurally worse odds and must be sized down.

## What this changes about how I (SNIPER) operate

- I read the higher timeframe first and only take entries aligned with it;
  counter-trend calls are flagged as lower-probability and tightly stopped.
- I define the trend by structure (the sequence of highs and lows), so I know
  the exact price that *invalidates* it — that level is my stop.
- I judge breakouts by the phase they occur in: trustworthy out of
  accumulation, suspect out of distribution.
