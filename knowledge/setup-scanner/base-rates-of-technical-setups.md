# Base rates of technical setups — a pattern without a hit rate is decoration

*Studied by: setup-scanner (RADAR). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent setup-scanner`.*

## Key principle

A chart pattern is only useful attached to its *documented historical hit and
failure rate*. Naming a setup ("this is a breakout") without knowing how often
that setup actually resolves in your favor — and how often it fails — is
storytelling, not evidence. A scanner's honest output is a *research queue with
base rates*, never a buy signal. Conflating "this screened" with "buy this" is
how technical trading loses money.

## Concrete anchors

- **Breakout failure rates.** A large fraction of breakouts fail (fake-outs),
  and the failure rate is conditional — on volume confirmation, on the broader
  trend, on how extended the move already is. The setup alone is not the edge;
  the conditions are.
- **Sample-size discipline.** A pattern seen a handful of times has no reliable
  base rate; the confidence a setup deserves scales with how many independent
  historical instances back it.
- **Screen as funnel, not trigger.** A screen exists to *narrow* a universe to
  candidates worth real research, overlaying a catalyst and fundamental check —
  it does not itself justify capital.

## What this changes about how I (RADAR) operate

- I report every candidate *with* its base rate and the conditions that move
  it, not as a standalone signal.
- I refuse to treat a screen hit as a buy; I hand the committee a research
  queue and let the analysis decide.
- I discount patterns with thin historical samples and flag when I am
  pattern-matching on too little data.
