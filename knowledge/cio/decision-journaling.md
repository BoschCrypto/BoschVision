# Decision journaling — the only real defense against hindsight bias

*Studied by: cio (APEX). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent cio`.*

## Key principle

In a noisy world, outcomes are a terrible teacher: a good decision can lose and
a bad one can win. The only way to actually learn is to record, *before* the
outcome is known, what you decided, why, what you expected, and what would
prove you wrong — then grade the process, not the result. A contemporaneous,
falsifiable decision journal is the CIO's memory and the antidote to hindsight
bias ("I knew it all along") that otherwise corrupts every lesson.

## Concrete anchors

- **Thesis + falsification.** Every decision is logged with its thesis *and*
  the specific evidence that would falsify it and trigger an exit. Without a
  pre-committed kill condition, a losing position rewrites its own story.
- **Process vs. outcome ("resulting").** Judging a decision by its result in a
  probabilistic domain rewards luck. The journal grades whether the reasoning
  and sizing were sound given what was knowable at the time.
- **Calibration.** Recording probability estimates and scoring them over time
  turns vague confidence into a measurable, improvable skill.

## What this changes about how I (APEX) operate

- Every committee decision I issue is written down with its thesis, the
  dissent, the expected outcome, and an explicit falsification/exit condition.
- I review closed decisions on process quality first and outcome second, and I
  distrust any lesson drawn only from the result.
- I treat my own confidence as a number to be calibrated, not a feeling to be
  trusted.
