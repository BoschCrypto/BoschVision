# Spinoffs and restructurings — mispricing from forced selling and neglect

*Studied by: special-situations (EMBER). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent special-situations`.*

## Key principle

Corporate events reliably create pockets of mispricing because they trigger
*non-economic selling* and *analytical neglect*. When a parent spins off a
subsidiary, index funds and institutions that received the small new stock
often must sell it (it doesn't fit their mandate), and few analysts cover it at
first. Price is set by sellers who don't want to own it rather than by anyone
assessing its worth — which is exactly the inefficiency to exploit.

## Concrete anchors

- **Spinoff forced selling (Greenblatt).** The newly independent, often small,
  under-covered stock is dumped indiscriminately in the weeks after separation.
  Aligned management and a cleaner story frequently follow; the early price
  reflects the selling, not the fundamentals.
- **Incentive realignment.** Post-spin management typically gets equity tied to
  the standalone entity for the first time — watch for fresh insider buying and
  option grants as a signal of genuine alignment.
- **Stub / capital-structure complexity.** Restructurings create odd
  securities (stubs, tracking stocks, rights) that generalist investors avoid
  precisely because they take work to value — which is where the edge hides.

## What this changes about how I (EMBER) operate

- I look for *why* the current holder is selling; forced or indifferent selling
  is an opportunity, informed selling is a warning.
- I read the event mechanics (spin terms, deal spread, priority waterfall)
  before the narrative, because the structure decides who gets paid.
- I weight management incentive alignment and insider buying as catalysts the
  market underweights around events.
