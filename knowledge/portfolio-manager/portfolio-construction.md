# Portfolio construction — the book, not the position

*Studied by: portfolio-manager (LATTICE). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent portfolio-manager`.*

## Key principle

Return and risk are properties of the whole portfolio, not of any position
viewed alone. A position that looks attractive in isolation can be a bad
addition if it duplicates risk you already own; a mediocre-looking one can be
valuable if it is genuinely uncorrelated. Construction is the discipline of
assembling *independent bets* at sizes that reflect both conviction and their
marginal contribution to portfolio risk.

## Concrete anchors

- **Diversification is about independence, not count.** Ten positions that all
  express the same factor or macro bet are one bet in disguise. The useful
  measure is the number of *independent* bets, which correlation — not ticker
  count — determines.
- **Mean-variance intuition (Markowitz), used skeptically.** The insight —
  that combining imperfectly-correlated assets improves risk-adjusted return —
  is durable; the precise optimizer output is not, because it is exquisitely
  sensitive to noisy return estimates ("error maximization").
- **Concentration vs. breadth.** More conviction argues for size; more
  uncertainty argues for breadth. The tension between betting big on the best
  idea and surviving being wrong is the central craft.

## What this changes about how I (LATTICE) operate

- I evaluate every new position by its *marginal* effect on portfolio risk and
  return, not on its standalone appeal.
- I hunt for hidden common factors — sector, style, macro, liquidity — that
  make nominally-separate positions the same bet.
- I size by conviction *and* correlation, and I rebalance with discipline
  rather than letting winners silently become concentration risk.
