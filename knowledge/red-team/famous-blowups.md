# Famous blow-ups — how theses die

*Studied by: red-team (TALON). A seed note demonstrating the format; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent red-team`.*

## Key principle

Almost every permanent capital loss rhymes with a prior one. The recurring
signature: **leverage + illiquidity + a correlation the model treated as
independent**, wrapped in a story that made the risk feel unique and therefore
invisible. Studying the deaths of past theses is the fastest way to recognize
the next one while it is still cheap to walk away.

## Concrete cases

- **LTCM (1998).** Nobel-laureate-run fund, ~25:1 balance-sheet leverage (far
  higher notionally via derivatives), running convergence trades that assumed
  spreads mean-revert and positions are independent. Russia's default made every
  "independent" spread widen *together*; leverage turned a model error into
  insolvency in weeks. Lesson: diversification across positions is an illusion
  when a single liquidity/flight-to-quality factor sits underneath them all.
- **Enron (2001).** Off-balance-sheet special-purpose entities hid debt and
  manufactured earnings; cash flow never matched reported profit. Red flags were
  in plain sight — related-party transactions, unintelligible disclosures, a CFO
  running the vehicles. Lesson: when earnings rise but operating cash doesn't
  follow, and the structure is too complex to explain simply, assume the
  complexity is the point.
- **Archegos (2021).** A family office took concentrated, swap-financed single-
  stock exposure so large and so hidden (total-return swaps kept it off public
  filings) that prime brokers couldn't see the aggregate. A few down days forced
  liquidation that cratered the underlying names. Lesson: hidden leverage and
  concentration are the same killer as LTCM in a new wrapper.

## What this changes about how I (TALON) operate

- On any bullish thesis, I explicitly ask: **what is the leverage, and where is
  the hidden correlation?** Independence assumptions are guilty until proven.
- I treat *earnings that outrun cash flow* and *structures too complex to
  explain in a sentence* as primary red flags, not footnotes.
- I anchor to base rates of ruin for the pattern (pre-revenue dilution, story
  stocks, leveraged concentration) rather than to the specific narrative, which
  is designed to feel exceptional.
