# Value-investing foundations — paying less than a business is worth

*Studied by: equity-analyst (LEDGER). Core seed note; expand via
`hf-bot study next --agent equity-analyst`.*

## Key principle

Price is what you pay; value is what you get. A share is a fractional claim on
a real business's future owner-earnings, not a ticker that moves. The whole
discipline reduces to two disciplines held at once: **estimate conservative
intrinsic value**, and **only act when price sits well below it** — the margin
of safety. Everything else (moats, growth, catalysts) is refinement on top of
this floor.

## Concrete anchors

- **Mr. Market (Graham).** The market is a manic-depressive business partner
  who quotes you a price every day. You are free to ignore him and obligated to
  exploit him — buy when he panics, sell when he is euphoric. His mood is an
  opportunity, never a signal about value.
- **Owner-earnings (Buffett).** Reported net income is an opinion; owner-
  earnings — cash a business throws off after the reinvestment needed to
  sustain its position — is closer to the truth. Divergence between the two is
  where both fraud and quality hide.
- **Margin of safety.** Because every estimate is wrong, the gap between a
  conservative value and the price paid is the only real protection. A great
  business bought with no margin of safety is still a bad investment.

## What this changes about how I (LEDGER) operate

- I lead with the business, not the chart: what does it earn for an owner, and
  is that durable? Only then do I look at price.
- I state intrinsic value as a conservative range and require price to sit
  below the low end before recommending action — no margin, no buy.
- I treat market volatility as inventory pricing, not information.

## Sources

Benjamin Graham & David Dodd, *Security Analysis* and *The Intelligent
Investor*; Warren Buffett, Berkshire shareholder letters (owner-earnings,
Mr. Market). Widely-documented public frameworks; no proprietary data.
