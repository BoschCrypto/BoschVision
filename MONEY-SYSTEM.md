### Three rules that do the real work
1. **The $2,000 transfer happens on the 2nd, before you can spend it.** Paying
   yourself last is why most people's savings rate is whatever is left over.
2. **Autopay credit cards at FULL STATEMENT BALANCE, never "minimum."** One
   missed payment at 25% APR wipes out a decade of card rewards.
3. **Never autopay a card from an account that could be short.** Keep one month of
   expenses in checking as the floor.

## 5. CREDIT CARDS

### The honest answer: two cards, not six.

You asked for a card per category. At **$520/month of cardable spend**, a
category-optimised wallet of five cards earns you maybe **$20–30 more per year**
than two cards — while adding five due dates, five annual-fee decisions, and five
chances to miss a payment. The complexity costs more than it pays.

| category | card | approx rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Groceries** | **Capital One SavorOne** (you have a Savor) | ~3% |
| **Restaurants** | **SavorOne** | ~3% |
| **Uber / Lyft** | **SavorOne** (rideshare often codes as travel/transit) | ~3% |
| **Streaming / entertainment** | **SavorOne** | ~3% |
| **Gas** | flat-2% card | 2% |
| **Travel** | flat-2% card | 2% |
| **Metro-North / commuting** | flat-2% card | 2% |
| **Everything else** | flat-2% card | 2% |

**Card 1 — Capital One SavorOne.** You already hold a Savor. **Check which
version**: the plain *Savor* carried a ~$95 fee; *SavorOne* is $0. If you have the
fee version, ask for a product change to SavorOne — same call, same account, fee
gone.

**Card 2 — a flat 2% card for everything else.** Pick one:
- **Fidelity Rewards Visa** — 2% deposited straight into your Fidelity account, $0 fee. Best fit given everything above.
- Citi Double Cash — 2%, $0 fee
- Wells Fargo Active Cash — 2%, $0 fee

**That's the whole wallet.** SavorOne for food and rides, the 2% card for
everything else, both on autopay at full balance.

### What NOT to do
- **No annual-fee travel cards.** Nothing with a fee earns it back on $520/mo.
- **No chasing sign-up bonuses** with minimum-spend requirements. They push you to
  spend more than you planned, and the spending always costs more than the bonus.
- **Never carry a balance.** A 25% APR beats every reward rate in existence. If you
  cannot pay it in full, the purchase was unaffordable.
- **Do not add a third card for gas.** At your volume it earns about $10/year.

### What credit cards are actually for, at 22
Not rewards — **building a credit file.** Two cards, used lightly, paid in full,
never closed, builds the history that gets you a mortgage at a good rate in ten
years. That is worth tens of thousands. The 3% on groceries is worth about $100.
Act accordingly: keep them open, keep utilisation under ~10%, autopay in full,
and stop thinking about them.
